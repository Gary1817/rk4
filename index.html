Theoretical Formulation: Undamped Nonlinear Pendulum

For the large-angle nonlinear pendulum simulated in our numerical experiments (bob mass $m$, rod length $L$, normalized to natural units $m = 1$, $L = 1$, and $g = 1$), the complete analytical progression from Lagrangian Mechanics to Hamiltonian Mechanics and the Symplectic Integrator (Velocity Verlet) is detailed below.

1. Lagrangian Formulation

  1. Generalized Coordinate and Energy Definitions:
    Select the unconstrained angular displacement $\theta$ as the generalized coordinate (with the zero of potential energy at the lowest equilibrium point):
    • Kinetic Energy: $$T = \frac{1}{2}m(L\dot{\theta})^2 = \frac{1}{2}\dot{\theta}^2 \quad (\text{setting } m = 1,\; L = 1)$$
    • Gravitational Potential Energy: $$V(\theta) = mgL(1 - \cos\theta) = 1 - \cos\theta \quad (\text{setting } m = 1,\; g = 1,\; L = 1)$$
  2. Constructing the Lagrangian: $$\mathcal{L}(\theta, \dot{\theta}) = T - V = \frac{1}{2}\dot{\theta}^2 - (1 - \cos\theta)$$
  3. Euler–Lagrange Equation: $$\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot{\theta}}\right) - \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \theta} = 0$$ Evaluating the partial derivatives yields: $$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot{\theta}} = \dot{\theta}, \qquad \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \theta} = -\sin\theta$$ Substituting these into the Euler–Lagrange equation gives the second-order nonlinear equation of motion: $$\ddot{\theta} + \sin\theta = 0$$

2. Hamiltonian Formulation

  1. Legendre Transformation & Canonical Momentum:
    Define the canonical angular momentum $p_\theta$ conjugate to $\theta$: $$p_\theta \equiv \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot{\theta}} = \dot{\theta}$$ (Note: In dimensionless natural units, the canonical momentum $p_\theta$ is numerically equal to the angular velocity $\omega$.)
  2. Constructing the Hamiltonian: $$H(\theta, p_\theta) = p_\theta \dot{\theta} - \mathcal{L}(\theta, \dot{\theta}) = p_\theta^2 - \left[\frac{1}{2}p_\theta^2 - (1 - \cos\theta)\right] = \frac{1}{2}p_\theta^2 + (1 - \cos\theta)$$ Because this is a separable Hamiltonian system, it decomposes cleanly into pure kinetic and pure potential energy terms: $$H(\theta, p_\theta) = T(p_\theta) + V(\theta)$$
  3. Hamilton's Canonical Equations: $$\begin{cases} \dfrac{d\theta}{dt} = \dfrac{\partial H}{\partial p_\theta} = p_\theta \\[12pt] \dfrac{dp_\theta}{dt} = -\dfrac{\partial H}{\partial \theta} = -\sin\theta \end{cases}$$ This coupled first-order system governs the phase-space state vector $\mathbf{y} = [\theta, p_\theta]^T$ implemented in our numerical solver.

3. Symplectic Integrator (Velocity Verlet): Derivation & Proof

3.1 Operator Splitting Derivation (Kick–Drift–Kick)

Time evolution in Hamiltonian mechanics can be expressed via the Liouville operator $\frac{d\mathbf{y}}{dt} = \{\mathbf{y}, H\} = (\hat{D}_T + \hat{D}_V)\mathbf{y}$, which splits into two exactly solvable sub-flows:

  • Pure Kinetic Flow (Drift — position advances linearly with constant momentum): $$\hat{D}_T: \quad \frac{d\theta}{dt} = p_\theta, \quad \frac{dp_\theta}{dt} = 0 \implies \begin{cases} \theta(t + \tau) = \theta(t) + \tau p_\theta(t) \\ p_\theta(t + \tau) = p_\theta(t) \end{cases}$$
  • Pure Potential Flow (Kick — momentum updates under torque at fixed position): $$\hat{D}_V: \quad \frac{d\theta}{dt} = 0, \quad \frac{dp_\theta}{dt} = -\sin\theta \implies \begin{cases} \theta(t + \tau) = \theta(t) \\ p_\theta(t + \tau) = p_\theta(t) - \tau \sin\theta(t) \end{cases}$$

To achieve second-order accuracy and time-reversibility, we apply Symmetric Strang Splitting over a single time step $h$ (Half-Kick $\rightarrow$ Full-Drift $\rightarrow$ Half-Kick):

  1. Half-step momentum update ($\tau = h/2$): $$p_{n+1/2} = p_n - \frac{h}{2}\sin\theta_n$$
  2. Full-step position update ($\tau = h$): $$\theta_{n+1} = \theta_n + h\,p_{n+1/2} = \theta_n + h\,p_n - \frac{h^2}{2}\sin\theta_n$$
  3. Half-step momentum update ($\tau = h/2$): $$p_{n+1} = p_{n+1/2} - \frac{h}{2}\sin\theta_{n+1} = p_n - \frac{h}{2}\big(\sin\theta_n + \sin\theta_{n+1}\big)$$

Defining the angular acceleration $\alpha(\theta) = -\sin\theta$, steps 2 and 3 yield the standard Velocity Verlet update equations:

$$\begin{aligned} \theta_{n+1} &= \theta_n + p_n h + \frac{1}{2}\alpha(\theta_n) h^2 \\ p_{n+1} &= p_n + \frac{1}{2}\big[\alpha(\theta_n) + \alpha(\theta_{n+1})\big]h \end{aligned}$$

3.2 Mathematical Proof of Symplecticity

In a two-dimensional phase space $(\theta, p_\theta)$, a numerical map $\Phi_h: (\theta_n, p_n) \mapsto (\theta_{n+1}, p_{n+1})$ is symplectic if and only if its Jacobian determinant equals unity ($\det(J) = 1$), ensuring exact preservation of phase-space area (discrete Liouville's theorem).

Evaluating the Jacobian matrix of each sub-step:

  • Step 1 (Half-Kick): $$J_1 = \frac{\partial(\theta_n, p_{n+1/2})}{\partial(\theta_n, p_n)} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -\frac{h}{2}\cos\theta_n & 1 \end{bmatrix} \implies \det(J_1) = 1$$
  • Step 2 (Full-Drift): $$J_2 = \frac{\partial(\theta_{n+1}, p_{n+1/2})}{\partial(\theta_n, p_{n+1/2})} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & h \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \implies \det(J_2) = 1$$
  • Step 3 (Half-Kick): $$J_3 = \frac{\partial(\theta_{n+1}, p_{n+1})}{\partial(\theta_{n+1}, p_{n+1/2})} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -\frac{h}{2}\cos\theta_{n+1} & 1 \end{bmatrix} \implies \det(J_3) = 1$$

By the chain rule, the total Jacobian determinant of the composite Velocity Verlet step is:

$$\det(J_{\text{Verlet}}) = \det(J_3)\det(J_2)\det(J_1) = 1 \times 1 \times 1 = 1$$
Physical Consequence (Shadow Hamiltonian Conservation):
Because $\det(J_{\text{Verlet}}) = 1$, Velocity Verlet strictly preserves phase-space area and conserves a nearby modified Shadow Hamiltonian $\tilde{H} = H + \mathcal{O}(h^2)$. Consequently, as demonstrated in Figure 4, the total energy $E(t)$ exhibits bounded periodic oscillations around $E_0 = 1.500$ over arbitrarily long integration times, avoiding the secular numerical damping inherent to non-symplectic RK4.
